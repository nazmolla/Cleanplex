# About Hayaa for Plex

**Hayaa for Plex** (formerly **Cleanplex**) is a self-hosted home server service that watches your Plex streams and, for the accounts you choose, skips or mutes the parts of a film that contain nudity or profanity.

!!! info "Independent project"
    Hayaa for Plex is an independent project; it works with Plex but is not made or endorsed by Plex. Only the name and look changed from Cleanplex: the repository (`github.com/nazmolla/Cleanplex`), Docker images (`ghcr.io/nazmolla/cleanplex`, `mnazmi/cleanplex`), settings, and data remain the same.

## What "Hayaa" means

**Hayaa** (Arabic **حياء**, said *ha-YAA*) has no exact English equivalent. It is closest to *modesty*, but it means more than how someone dresses: it is the natural sense of decency that makes a person look away, lower their voice, or feel uncomfortable when something private is put on display. In Arab and Muslim cultures it is seen as a virtue, part of good character, and something parents hope to pass on to their children.

The icon shows exactly that: a woman drawing her scarf across her face, the instinctive gesture of hayaa.

## The vision

Films are one of the things families most enjoy together, and also one of the hardest to share: a single scene can make a whole evening uncomfortable. Hayaa for Plex exists so that people can watch what they love, with whoever they love, without that worry.

- **Your home, your standards.** You decide, for each person who watches, what gets skipped or muted. Nothing is imposed, and nothing changes for viewers you do not filter.
- **Private by design.** Your library is scanned on your own server. Titles, file names and frames never leave it.
- **Better together.** Scanning takes hours, so servers can choose to share what they found through [Hayaa community sync](hayaa-sync.md). The more people share, the faster everyone's library becomes watchable, and the more accurate each segment gets.
- **Free and open.** No subscriptions, no accounts with a streaming service, and the code is open for anyone to read.
